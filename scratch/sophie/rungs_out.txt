Here is the case for each, and then my call.

For So400m. At 512 it is ahead of large on every column the paper reports, not most: 84.3 against 83.5 on ImageNet, 79.1 against 77.8 on ImageNet v2, 86.2 against 84.6 on ObjectNet, 80.5 against 79.6 on ten-shot, 48.3 against 47.4 on XM3600. And the strongest single number for it is not in that table at all but in Table 8, where ten-shot Dollar Street is 40.8 against 32.5, plus 26%, and GeoDE country is 47.6 against 42.5. That is few-shot transfer of hard, diverse, long-tailed categories, which is the closest published analogue to booru tagging that exists in this paper.

For large. It costs 3.55x base in FLOPs against So400m's 5.06x, so So400m is 1.42x the compute of large for a gain of one to two percent on the columns where the towers' image-text quality is measured. Large is 316.74M vision parameters against 428.77M. At 512 it fits a batch of 20 in our 24.5 GB card while So400m wants 16 or less, and every hyperparameter we have ever validated was measured at batch 48, so the further we drift from that the more of a retune we owe. And our own history says a size rung is not where our wins come from: DINOv3-B to SigLIP2-B at identical size was plus 21% macro, while a whole rung of tower scale in this paper is worth one to two points of zero-shot.

My call is large, and the shape of the argument is that large captures most of the size gain and So400m asks for 42% more compute for the remainder. The one thing that would change it is if the Dollar Street column generalises to us better than ImageNet does, because that plus 26% is much larger than any of the other gaps and hard long-tailed categories are what we are actually predicting. That is a real possibility and I do not want to wave it away, so the way to test it is not to guess: run large at 600k, and if it gains more than the band over base, the extra 42% for So400m becomes a second arm rather than a leap. If large comes in flat, So400m at 1.42x large on flat evidence is the wrong bet.

## The 384 idea, which is a bigger lever than the tower

You spotted the right thing and it is worth more than the tower choice. At 384 the token count falls from 1024 to 576, and because attention is quadratic in tokens while the MLP is linear, the saving is better than the ratio of tokens:

| tower | tokens at 512 | relative compute at 512 | tokens at 384 | relative compute at 384 | speedup |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| B/16 | 1024 | 8.05 | 576 | 3.74 | 2.15x |
| L/16 | 1024 | 12.89 | 576 | 6.19 | 2.08x |
| So400m/16 | 1024 | 15.96 | 576 | 7.66 | 2.08x |

So 2.1x, not 1.78x, on every tower at once, and the paper says it is nearly free: large at 384 is 83.1 against 83.5 at 512, So400m is 84.1 against 84.3, base is 80.6 against 81.2. The 384 checkpoints are not the 512 ones fed smaller images either, they are separately adapted from the 256 checkpoint at 95% of training, so they are a first-class model rather than a downscale.

In our own pipeline this is nearly a one-line change. `wdtag/cache.py` line 26 is `SIZE = 512`, and everything that matters imports it: the streamer's ring buffer, the nested validation cache, the cache builder, and the validator. Changing it to 384 and rebuilding the caches is the whole edit, and the caches get 44% smaller as a side effect, which at our current 100.3 GB is 56 GB.

Two caveats before anyone gets excited. The 512 checkpoint and the 384 checkpoint are different weights files, so this experiment needs `siglip2-base-patch16-384` downloaded, and the DINOv3 side needs its positional embedding interpolated from 1024 positions to 576, which DINOv3 supports but which our code has never exercised at a resolution other than the default. And the risk is specific to us: anime images carry tag-relevant detail in fingers, hair accessories, text and small props, and ImageNet-style object classification may be more forgiving of 576 tokens than a booru vocabulary is. The paper cannot answer that, only our 50k can.
