## Traps found today, in the order they bit

The pinned ring in `wdtag/stream.py` was sized for a consumer holding one batch. Gradient accumulation holds `grad_accum` batches, the ring overwrapped, and the producer wrote into batches the trainer had not consumed. Two runs of the same config then agreed for fifty steps and drifted apart at a random step. Fixed with `hold` on `TarStream` and `ring = prefetch + hold + 1`; the trainer passes `hold=accum`. After the fix, pause and resume with accumulation is 288/288 tensors bit-identical.

`grad_checkpoint_blocks` did nothing on an OFT-only run. Its gate required `unfreeze_blocks > 0`, but the graph runs through the OFT rotations even with every weight frozen, so activations are kept regardless. Now gated on `n_unfrozen > 0 or n_oft > 0` and wrapping the whole encoder in the OFT case. Untested on the card as of this writing, which is why arm 35 is the first thing to launch and watch.

Feeding `siglip2-large-patch16-512` weights at 384 fails with a position embedding broadcast error, because that tower carries 1024 positions for a 32x32 patch grid. The fix is not interpolation, it is the native checkpoint `I:\Models\siglip2-large-patch16-384`, which is what the 384 configs now point at.

Scoring `f63f6f54` at an intermediate checkpoint overwrote `runs/f63f6f54/validation.json`. It now reads step 30000 again because I rescored from the cached probabilities, and the intermediate score is kept as `validation_step012000.json`. Use `--out` next time.

Batch 48 at 384 without checkpointing sits at 23.5 GB and collapses to 3 img/s on a 24.5 GB card. It does not OOM, it just becomes unusable, which is worse because it looks like it is working.
