Rung one, no cache, is what we have been doing. Every experiment pays the backbone, 90 to 120 img/s for a full training step, and one experiment is a night.

Rung two is the pooled embedding, one vector per image. For SigLIP2 that is 768 floats, 1536 bytes in fp16, 8.2 GB for all 5.3 million images, and one forward pass at 270 img/s costs about 5.5 hours. This is what I built and tested today.

Rung three is all four poolings rather than just the class token: the class token, the mean over patches, the max over patches and the std over patches, concatenated. That is 3072 floats, 6144 bytes, 32.7 GB, and it costs exactly the same forward pass as rung two because the pooling happens after the trunk. The production model used cls only. The max pool is the interesting one, because max asks whether a concept appears anywhere in the image, which is exactly the question a tag decision asks.

Rung four is the patch tokens themselves, 1024 tokens of 768 dimensions, 1.57 MB per image, which is 8.4 TB for the corpus and therefore not a corpus at all. It is only viable for a subset: 25000 images is 39 GB, which we can afford. This is the only rung that lets you train an attention pool or a small transformer that decides where to look, rather than how to weight a vector that already averaged the image.
