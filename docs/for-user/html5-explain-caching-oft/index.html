a visual note · feature caching and OFT

Frozen is not cacheable

An adapter inside a tower changes its output. That one fact decides the cost of your two-tower design.

01 · the step

The step that costs everything

Every optimizer step runs all twelve blocks again. At 130 images/s, one pass is 11.4 hours.

02 · caching

Cache the tower's output once

A cache is valid only if the tower's output cannot change during the run.

03 · the break

Frozen is not the condition

A trainable rotation inside the tower changes the output. The stored vector becomes a stale answer.

04 · OFT

What OFT actually is

A learned orthogonal rotation inside the tower, block-diagonal at 128 rows, stored as a small skew matrix.

05 · after pooling

A rotation after pooling is absorbable

W(Rx) = (WR)x. Still one linear map; the classifier could learn it without orthogonality.

06 · three regimes

Three regimes, three prices

Only A is cacheable. B and C still walk backward through frozen blocks to reach their rotations.

07 · the design

Your design, end to end

One 512 image, two normalisations, 24 blocks, two rotations and two unfrozen blocks, one 1536-d head.

08 · the payoff

What the second tower buys

Frozen concat measured +32% macro over the better tower with a linear head, and only +1.6% once either tower gets a bottleneck. A fine-tuned DINOv3 may already fill that gap.

09 · cheat sheet

The one-condition rule

Three designs, one test. The same invariant decides all of them.
