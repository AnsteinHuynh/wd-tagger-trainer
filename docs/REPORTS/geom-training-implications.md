# What this means for training on squashed 512×512

1. Every image is used, but the median one is stretched 31–41%. Median source `640×841–853`; squash to 512² applies 0.80× horizontally and 0.61× vertically, so faces and bodies are systematically ~30–40% wider than in the source art. Only 5.3% of the 10000 training images are `d = 1.000` (square, no distortion); 76.3% are distorted >1.25×.
2. The tail is worse than the median. 1996/10000 (19.96%) >1.5×, 862 (8.62%) >1.75×, 288 (2.88%)
> 2×, 42 (0.42%) >3×; worst is `462×9066` → d = 19.6, vertical scale 20× horizontal. These images contribute "impossible anatomy" gradients that no label contradicts.
3. A consistent bias, not a train/test shift. Test geometry matches (median 0.750 vs 0.750; `d > 1.5` 22.30% vs 19.96%; p95 aspect 1.63 vs 1.60) and the cache builder applies the identical squash, so the model learns shape in warped space and is scored in the same warped space.
4. Squash buys short-side resolution and pays in anisotropy. For the 85% with min side exactly 640, squash maps the short side to 512 at 0.8× and the long side at `512/h`. Letterboxing would scale both axes by `512/max(w,h)` — for the median `640×853` that is 0.6×, leaving the short side at 384 px. Squash keeps ~33% more short-side linear resolution (512 vs 384) and pays 1.33× anisotropy; the alternative keeps shape and pays 26.7% blank canvas on average (median 29.1%, p95 45.0%).
5. Image sizes span 0.06–6.70 in aspect and 100–8231 px in height. 14.9% are native-small (`min side < 640`) and 3.98% get upscaled horizontally by the squash (up to 5.12×), so part of the set is low-resolution and interpolated — squash adds blur on top of distortion.
6. Annotations cannot police any of this. No tag describes stretch/aspect (`aspect`, `disproportion`, `squished`, … all absent), so labels give no geometry signal and the CSV of true `w,h` exists because a tag-based audit is impossible. The only geometry-adjacent labels are `letterboxed` (0.67%), `pillarboxed` (0.08%) and `border`/`white_border` (1.89%/1.12%): learnable as appearance, and they survive the squash (bars stay bars, just squashed). Nothing removes bars, so there is no bar-removal to detect; the only "padding" is inherited from source art at ~1–2% prevalence.
