# The strongest counter-evidence, and what it implies

The 692.6M bar, with a simpler and smaller head (35.15M linear over a single pooled 2816-d vector), beats us 0.5579 vs 0.2815 on macro. Our head is bigger (38.33M over a 3072-d concat of four pooled statistics) and loses. Head capacity is not the differentiator, and the measured bound closes the loophole: even an oracle that tunes a threshold per tag on the same 50k reaches only 0.3879, so no re-scoring of this checkpoint — per-class offsets, weight norm, temperature, prior correction — can reach the bar. The ~0.17 remaining gap is in the scores.

That is why the shortlist is built as it is: items 1 and 2 change what the head learns (prior correction trained in, negative-tolerant bias), item 3 adds information the features do not carry (label semantics), and item 5 is the only "more capacity" option, ranked last, expected ~+0.01. The head-architecture question is largely settled — ML-Decoder's own ablation says a query head is worth 0.8 mAP over GAP at 9600 labels — so the question is not "which head" but "which training signal makes rare-tag scores cross 0.40".

Consistent with `docs/bar-convnextv2-huge.md`: the bar reaches 97% of its final macro in epoch 1, so the gap is per-sample efficiency (capacity/architecture/data), not schedule. Head+loss work should be worth single-digit points of macro, not a factor of two. If the frequency-binned analysis (item 4) comes back flat, items 1–3 are polishing and the effort belongs in the backbone (ViT-L, more unfrozen blocks, better augmentation/mixup) — out of scope here but where the 0.17 lives.
