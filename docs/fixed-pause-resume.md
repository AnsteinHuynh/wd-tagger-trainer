Found it. It was not rounding and it was not a race in the kernels — it was my own bug in the streaming ring, and it would have silently fed the real arm corrupted images.

The streamer preallocates a ring of pinned batches and decides how many to keep with prefetch + 2. That arithmetic quietly assumes the trainer holds exactly one batch: at most four in the queue, one in hand, one being written, six in the ring. Gradient accumulation makes the trainer hold the whole group, so with three micro-batches the live count is four queued plus three held plus one being written, eight against a ring of six. The producer laps and writes into slots the trainer has not consumed yet, so a batch comes back with some of its images replaced by later ones and their labels. That is why the symptom was so slippery: two runs agree for fifty or sixty steps, then drift apart at a random step, with all seventy nine trainable tensors differing at once and the gap growing smoothly. It is timing-dependent data corruption, and it could never appear without accumulation because there the consumer holds one batch.

The fix is a hold parameter on TarStream with ring = prefetch + hold + 1, and the trainer passes hold=accum. At the default it is the same six slots as before, so no existing arm changes.

Things I ruled out first, each with a measurement: stream order under bursty pulls, hashed over three hundred batches in two processes and identical every time; batch four on its own without accumulation, bit-identical over a hundred and twenty steps; a pinned cuBLAS workspace, which did not help; serialised kernel launches, which did not help either.

After the fix, all of it under --deterministic: two independent accumulation runs are 288/288 bit-identical at steps 20, 40, 60, 80, 100 and 300. Pause at step 151 and resume is 288/288 bit-identical to the uninterrupted control, resuming by skipping 1812 samples, which is 151 groups of twelve. A stream length that is not a whole number of accumulated steps finishes and logs the short step. .PAUSE before the first step exits without writing a single checkpoint. A checkpoint truncated to half its length is reported unreadable and the resume falls back to the previous one, then carries on from step 100.
